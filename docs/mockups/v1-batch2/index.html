Good Company · V1 mockups, batch 2

Thirteen issues, one band each, for the second V1 batch (docs/issues/V1_BATCH_2.md). They use the app’s own colours, type and components, follow the decisions dated 2026-11-13 and 2026-11-14 in docs/PRODUCT_BRIEF.md, and use the open-question defaults written in the issue plan. Sample people are the app’s demo circle. These are proposals for review, not renders of the real app.

I1 · owner request 2026-11-14, D2

“Pick from contacts” opens the contact list

On iOS and the Android sideload build, the add menu has one way in from the phone: the contact list with checkboxes, so several people come in at once. If contacts access is refused, the system picker is the fallback. The Play build keeps the system picker until Google Play’s READ_CONTACTS policy is checked.

I2 · F10, F4, F19, F14

Friend page basics: name, photo, birthday

A name and a photo set in the app win over the contact’s and survive a sync; both can go back to the contact’s. Photos go through a crop editor. Birthday reminders are on by default, and the year is optional: no year, no age.

I3 · F1, F3

AI hidden until it’s on · activity list

A fresh install shows nothing AI: no Ideas tab, no “Things to talk about”, no AI buttons. One switch in Settings turns it all on and reveals the key field. People who already have a key keep AI on. Settings also chooses and orders the activities the log sheet offers.

I4 · F16

Shared tags for likes and dislikes

Tags are one list shared by everyone and by every taste field. While typing, matches from other people show first; with nothing typed, the most used ones do. Settings renames, merges duplicates and deletes. “Sushi” can be one person’s like and another’s dislike.

I5 · F6, F18, gap #12

Links between people, and how they relate to you

People can be linked to each other (family, romantic, friend, colleague), and every link reads correctly from both sides. Several romantic links can be on file at once; each can have a start date (an anniversary to remember) and, for an ex, an end date. Separately, each person can say how they relate to you; everyone with a family relation forms a built-in Family circle.

I6 · F9, F10

Kids are people

A kid added on someone’s page becomes a person with their own page, birthday reminder, tastes and gifts, linked to their parent. “Show in my list” is on for family and off for friends’ kids. In the list they look like anyone else, with a line under the name; they are not nested under the parent.

I7 · F7

Automatic kinship

Kinship is worked out from the links (my brother’s daughter is my niece) and proposed, never set silently: one tap confirms. Ambiguous cases (half-siblings, step-family, in-laws through an ex) ask. Search understands “my nieces and nephews”, “my cousins”.

I8 · F8, F17

Gift lists per occasion

A list per occasion (“Christmas 2026”, a Secret Santa) with people added by circle or by search. Each person shows their age, saved ideas (including ones shared from a shop), past gifts and, with AI on, suggestions. Each gift moves from to buy → bought → given; “given” lands in the person’s gift history. Optional budget per person, totals at the top, and “duplicate last year’s list”.

I9 · F20

Plans with a circle, and recurring plans

A plan can be with a whole circle as well as with people, and it can repeat (“climbing every 15 days”). Each occurrence can go to the calendar and is notified on the day. The day after, “Did you meet?” opens a group check-in with the activity filled in and the circle’s members unticked, so you tick who came.

I10 · F11

Named places, GPS only

A check-in keeps GPS coordinates only; no place-search or map service is called. At a new spot the app asks “What place is this?”. Later check-ins within about 100 m get that name by themselves, and the log sheet suggests the people you’re usually with there. Everything stays on the device.

I11 · D3, F2

Calendar suggestions

Opt-in and off by default. You choose which calendars the app may read (personal yes, work no). Events are matched to people by attendee email and by name in the title, on the device only; they are never sent anywhere or to the AI. Each suggestion is yes, no, or never for that series. Android and iOS; not on the web.

I12 · gap #13, #28, F6

Network map and dashboard

The map puts you at the centre, with people linked by family, romantic and other links (I5) and by “introduced by”. Colour follows the circle, size follows warmth (hidden when “Show warmth” is off). The dashboard sums up warmth per circle, the trend, who is cooling and how often you meet.

I13 · F2, F3

“We met” opens a small panel

Every “We met” in the app opens the same small panel instead of logging silently. Today is preselected and Save is right there, so logging today is still two taps. Yesterday and “pick a date” log past meetups, the activity is one icon, and the note is optional. No hidden gestures. “More details” opens the full Log sheet.
