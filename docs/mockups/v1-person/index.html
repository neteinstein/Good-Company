A person’s page: for every day, with editing on its own

Proposal for review (2026-11-14), after the People tab review (#86). Today a person’s page is about 22 cards, nearly all of them always editable: tag editors, occasion chips, a × on every circle, update and moment, “Related to you?” always asking, the settings at the bottom and Delete next to Back. Someone who opens Ana to call her, or to remember her kids’ names, scrolls through a form. The proposal: the page is for looking and acting, the family sits around the photo, the content splits into three tabs (Summary, About, History), and the pencil switches to editing for what rarely changes. Tastes, notes and conversations stay editable without it.

Proposal · the top and the Summary tab

Family around the photo, four actions, then three tabs

The top shows who she is and who her people are, and the four actions you use. Under it, three tabs: Summary (opens by default: what’s coming, what to talk about, and the tastes and notes you edit right there), About and History. Scrolling shrinks the top into a bar with her name, and the tabs stay pinned under it.

Proposal · About and History

Who she is, and what happened

About is the reference: work, how you met, family, gifts, occasions, moments, saved things, blogs. Read-only until the pencil; a card with nothing in it doesn’t show, and “Complete Ana’s profile” lists what’s missing. History is the timeline and the conversations, which stay usable here (import a chat, log something).

Proposal · edit mode

The pencil edits what rarely changes

Same screen, same tab, same scroll position. The About cards show their editors (empty ones too), the × buttons appear, and “Related to you?”, the preferred channel, the rhythm controls, notification settings and Remove live here. Tastes, notes and conversations don’t need it. “Done” goes back. Everything still saves as you type, as today.
